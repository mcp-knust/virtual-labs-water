Session 08 · Organic pollution

Dissolved Oxygen & BOD5

Dissolved oxygen is the life-support parameter of a water body and the controlling variable in activated sludge. BOD5 measures how much oxygen bacteria will consume oxidising the organic matter in a sample over five days — the standard index of biodegradable load.

60 min Winkler titration BOD kinetics DO sag

Objectives

  • Determine dissolved oxygen by the Winkler (iodometric) method and correct for sample volume.
  • Compute BOD5 from dilution, initial and final DO, with a seeded blank correction.
  • Derive the ultimate BOD and the rate constant from a BOD progression.
  • Apply the Streeter–Phelps equation to predict the oxygen sag downstream of a discharge.

Theory

Dissolved oxygen by the Winkler method

Manganese(II) sulphate and alkaline iodide–azide are added to a sealed sample. The manganese is oxidised by dissolved oxygen to a higher oxide, which then oxidises iodide to iodine in acid solution. The liberated iodine is titrated with sodium thiosulphate, with starch as indicator:

Mn2+ + 2OH− + ½O2 → MnO2 + H2O
MnO2 + 2I− + 4H+ → Mn2+ + I2 + 2H2O
I2 + 2S2O32− → 2I− + S4O62−
DO (mg/L) = (Vthiosulphate × N × 8000) / Vsample

With 0.025 N thiosulphate and a 200 mL aliquot, 1.00 mL of titrant equals 1.00 mg/L of DO, which is why the method is normally set up with those numbers.

Biochemical oxygen demand

BOD is a bioassay, not a chemical measurement. A diluted sample is incubated at 20 °C in the dark for five days, and the oxygen consumed is measured. Because the reaction follows first-order kinetics with respect to the remaining biodegradable matter:

BODt = L0(1 − e−k t)   →   L0 = BOD5 / (1 − e−5k)

For the diluted sample with a seeded blank, where P is the volume fraction of sample:

BOD5 = [ (D1 − D2) − (B1 − B2)(1 − P) ] / P
Valid depletion — at least 2 mg/L consumed and at least 1 mg/L remaining; otherwise choose a different dilution.
Nitrification — ammonia oxidisers also consume oxygen; inhibited with ATU if carbonaceous BOD is required.
k — deoxygenation constant, base e, about 0.1–0.3 d−1 for domestic sewage at 20 °C.

Oxygen sag

Downstream of a discharge, oxygen is consumed by the waste and replenished from the atmosphere. The Streeter–Phelps solution gives the deficit D at travel time t:

Dt = (kd L0 / (kr − kd)) (e−kdt − e−krt) + D0 e−krt

and the critical (minimum) oxygen occurs at

tc = &frac{1}{kr − kd} ln[ (kr/kd) (1 − D0(kr−kd)/(kdL0)) ]

Apparatus and reagents

Apparatus

  • BOD bottles 300 mL, glass-stoppered, with ground necks
  • Burette 10 mL with 0.05 mL graduations
  • Incubator at 20 ± 1 °C, dark
  • DO meter for comparison, magnetic stirrer

Reagents

  • Manganese(II) sulphate solution
  • Alkali–iodide–azide reagent
  • Concentrated sulphuric acid
  • Sodium thiosulphate 0.025 N, starch indicator
  • Dilution water, seeded and ATU-inhibited

Procedure

  1. Fill a BOD bottle carefully to avoid bubbles; stopper with no air space. Fix oxygen immediately by adding 1 mL each of manganese sulphate and alkali–iodide below the surface.
  2. Stopper and invert several times; allow the precipitate to settle, then invert again.
  3. Add 1 mL concentrated sulphuric acid, stopper and mix until the precipitate dissolves.
  4. Titrate 200 mL of the solution with 0.025 N thiosulphate to pale yellow, add starch, and continue to the first disappearance of blue.
  5. For BOD: prepare at least three dilutions and a seeded blank, measure D1 in one bottle of each, and incubate the rest at 20 °C for five days in the dark.
  6. Measure D2 after five days; accept only dilutions with 2 mg/L depletion and 1 mg/L residual DO.

Interpretation

Domestic sewage has a BOD5 of roughly 200–400 mg/L; a treated effluent should be below 50 mg/L to discharge to a water body (GS 175-1), and below 20 mg/L for discharge to a sensitive receiving water. The BOD/COD ratio indicates biodegradability: above 0.5 the waste is readily biodegradable and biological treatment will work; below 0.3 a significant fraction is recalcitrant and chemical or physical treatment is needed in addition.

Invalid results are common. If depletion is under 2 mg/L the dilution was too strong and the result is dominated by analytical noise; if final DO is under 1 mg/L the dilution was too weak and the bacteria may have been oxygen-limited. Both cases require a new dilution series, not a correction factor.

Review questions

1. A 200 mL aliquot requires 7.40 mL of 0.025 N thiosulphate. What is the DO?

DO = (7.40 × 0.025 × 8000)/200 = 7.40 mg/L. With these standard conditions the titrant volume in millilitres equals the DO in mg/L directly, which is why the method is set up this way. At 20 °C saturation is 9.09 mg/L, so the water is at about 81% saturation.

2. A diluted sample with P = 0.02 has D₁ = 8.4 and D₂ = 4.1 mg/L; the seeded blank depleted 0.2 mg/L. Compute BOD₅.

BOD5 = [(8.4 − 4.1) − 0.2(1 − 0.02)]/0.02 = [4.3 − 0.196]/0.02 = 205 mg/L. The dilution is valid: depletion is 4.3 mg/L (above 2) and final DO is 4.1 mg/L (above 1). Note how the seed correction is scaled by the fraction of dilution water, not by P.

3. Why is BOD measured at 20 °C in the dark?

Temperature sets the rate: a standard temperature makes results comparable between laboratories, and 20 °C is close to natural water temperatures in temperate climates while still allowing measurable depletion in five days. The dark prevents photosynthesis, which would add oxygen and mask the consumption being measured, and prevents algal growth.

4. A river has L₀ = 20 mg/L, k_d = 0.22/d, k_r = 0.45/d and no initial deficit. When and where is the DO minimum?

With D₀ = 0, tc = ln(kr/kd)/(kr−kd) = ln(2.045)/0.23 = 0.715/0.23 ≈ 3.1 days. The deficit there is (0.22 × 20/0.23)(e−0.68 − e−1.40) ≈ 19.1 × (0.507 − 0.247) ≈ 5.0 mg/L. If saturation is 8.5 mg/L the minimum DO is about 3.5 mg/L, below the 5 mg/L needed for most fish — the discharge needs more treatment. At 0.3 m/s the critical point is roughly 80 km downstream.
