Interactive laboratories

Two instruments, built for projection and for practice

Both laboratories are single-file applications. They open full screen, work without a network connection, and keep every calculation inside the browser tab.

Materials modelling

Clean Water Through Materials Modelling

An eighteen-slide teaching deck on molecular dynamics, density functional theory and machine learning for water treatment, with two embedded applications: a factor analysis of capture efficiency against temperature, pressure, pH and competing ions, and a neural network that learns to screen candidate materials.

Quantum science

Quantum Science & Technology

A ten-station laboratory that starts with vectors and matrices and ends with a variational quantum eigensolver. Every station is manipulable: build electron configurations for all 118 elements, apply real complex gates to a qubit, and compare measurement correlations across two entangled qubits.

How the laboratory works

Brief, instrument, discussion

Sessions are designed to be run in a supervised class or independently, at the pace of the group. Nothing is uploaded and no account is required.

Read the brief

Each session opens with its scope and the measurement it is built around. Where the apparatus is still in preparation, the brief is what is currently published.

Operate the instrument

Interactive stations respond to your own inputs — matrices, element symbols, gate sequences, or simulation settings — and recompute the result as you change them.

Discuss the result

Every station is built to produce a result worth arguing about: a measurement, a configuration, a correlation, or a trend that changes when a condition changes.

About the laboratory

Mathematical and Computational Physics at KNUST

The laboratory supports teaching and outreach in the Mathematical and Computational Physics programme of the Department of Physics, KNUST. Its sessions follow the modelling approach the programme teaches: describe a system with the right equations, compute it, and then check the computed behaviour against the physical world.

Two public-facing laboratories are published in full — a materials-modelling deck prepared for World Rivers Day on clean water and sustainable livelihoods, and a quantum science laboratory that carries a class from vector algebra to a variational quantum eigensolver. The remaining pathways publish their session briefs while their instruments are prepared.

Every page on this site is static. Simulations execute in your browser and no measurement, input or identifier leaves the device.
